Exact Feasibility Certification and Optimal Responsibility Allocation for Multi-Robot CBF Safety Filters

Chandan Kumar Sah1    Jishnu Keshavan1
1 Department of Mechanical Engineering, Indian Institute of Science
Paper Code Video BibTeX

Certificate-optimal responsibility allocation resolves multi-robot CBF infeasibility by redistributing shared safety constraints based on each agent’s available control authority.

Motivation

Control Barrier Functions (CBFs) provide a powerful mechanism for enforcing safety through online optimization. For a multi-robot system, however, several safety constraints may compete for the limited control authority of different robots, causing the CBF safety filter to become infeasible.

The usual outcome is simply a binary signal: feasible or infeasible. But infeasibility alone does not answer the questions that matter in practice: Which interaction is responsible? Which robot lacks sufficient control authority? And how should the shared safety constraints be distributed among the robots?

This motivates an exact feasibility certificate that separates the demand imposed by the safety constraints from the control authority supplied by the actuators. Rather than merely detecting infeasibility, the certificate reveals its source and quantifies how much each agent can contribute.

This perspective turns CBF infeasibility from a failure signal into a diagnostic and allocation problem: identify the responsible interaction, determine where additional actuator authority would be useful, and optimally distribute shared constraints so that the worst local feasibility margin is maximized.

Why feasibility alone is not enough. CBF infeasibility is not merely a binary failure: the proposed certificate identifies the source of infeasibility and enables principled responsibility allocation among interacting robots.

Central Research Question

"Can safety guarantees be recovered when surrounding obstacles are allowed to maneuver within known physical limits?"

A New Safety Framework: Adversarial Control Barrier Functions

The failure of DPCBF arises because the classical CBF condition certifies safety only under the assumed obstacle dynamics. Once obstacles are allowed to accelerate or turn, satisfying the nominal barrier condition no longer guarantees that the true barrier dynamics remain safe.

To address this limitation, we introduce the Adversarial Control Barrier Function (A-CBF), a new validity framework that explicitly accounts for bounded obstacle maneuvers. Instead of certifying safety for a single predicted obstacle trajectory, an A-CBF guarantees that the barrier condition remains valid against every admissible obstacle maneuver within the capability set $\mathcal{F}$.

This perspective naturally leads to a two-player zero-sum differential game. The robot selects a control input to maximize safety, while the obstacle acts as an adversary that chooses the maneuver within $\mathcal{F}$ that minimizes the barrier derivative. An A-CBF is valid only if the robot can satisfy the barrier condition against this worst-case disturbance, i.e.,

$$\boxed{\ \color{red}{\min_{\mathbf{d}\in\mathcal{F}} \; \max_{\mathbf{u}\in\mathcal{U}} \; \Big(\dot h(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{u},\mathbf{d}) + \alpha(h)\Big) \ge 0}\ }$$

Equivalently, for every admissible obstacle maneuver there exists a control input that preserves the barrier condition. AR-DPCBF is then constructed as a geometry-preserving barrier that satisfies this adversarial validity criterion while retaining the computational efficiency of the original DPCBF.

Proposed Algorithm: AR-DPCBF

AR-DPCBF extends DPCBF by explicitly accounting for bounded obstacle maneuvers. Rather than assuming constant obstacle velocity, each obstacle is modeled as an adversary whose acceleration and turning rate are bounded by the capability set $\mathcal{F} = \{\, |a_{obs}| \le a_{obs,\max},\ |\omega_{obs}| \le \omega_{obs,\max} \,\}$, which is summarized by the scalar capability parameter:

$$\boxed{\color{red}{\kappa := a_{obs,\max} + v_{obs,\max}\cdot \omega_{obs,\max}}} \qquad [\text{m/s}^2]$$

The parameter $\kappa$ upper-bounds the obstacle's maneuvering authority and determines the amount by which the nominal DPCBF certificate must be contracted. AR-DPCBF preserves the original parabolic barrier and contracts its parameters according to the worst-case maneuver permitted by $\kappa$:

$$\text{DPCBF} \quad \color{red}\boxed{{h \;=\; \tilde v_{rel,x} + \lambda\, \tilde v_{rel,y}^2 + \mu}} \qquad \color{black} \lambda = k_\lambda\dfrac{d}{\|v_{rel}\|}, \qquad \mu = k_\mu d$$ $$\text{AR-DPCBF} \quad \color{red}\boxed{{h^* \;=\; \tilde v_{rel,x} + \lambda^*\, \tilde v_{rel,y}^2 + \mu^*}} \qquad \color{black} \lambda^* = \lambda - \dfrac{\kappa}{\gamma a_{\max}\|v_{rel}\|}, \qquad \mu^* = \mu - \dfrac{\kappa d}{\gamma \|v_{rel}\|}$$

As the admissible obstacle capability $\kappa$ increases, the AR-DPCBF parabola widens, enlarging the adversarial unsafe set $\{h^*<0\}$ and correspondingly shrinking the certified safe set $\{h^* \ge 0\}$. Consequently, the AR-DPCBF safe set forms a conservative inner approximation of the nominal DPCBF safe set, reserving sufficient safety margin to guarantee validity against all admissible obstacle maneuvers. When $\kappa=0$, the contraction vanishes, the adversarial and nominal safe sets coincide, and AR-DPCBF exactly recovers the original DPCBF.

Abstract

Adversarial-Robust Dynamic Parabolic Control Barrier Functions (AR-DPCBF) extend Dynamic Parabolic Control Barrier Functions (DPCBFs) to dynamic environments with maneuvering obstacles. Unlike DPCBF, which assumes constant obstacle velocity, AR-DPCBF models obstacle maneuvers through a bounded-adversary framework and derives a geometry-preserving robust safety certificate with formal guarantees. The proposed approach introduces Adversarial Control Barrier Functions (A-CBFs), closed-form parameter contractions, explicit feasibility conditions, and an online obstacle capability estimator. Two soft-constrained variants further improve feasibility in cluttered environments while retaining nominal DPCBF safety. Extensive simulations demonstrate significant reductions in barrier violations and collisions compared with DPCBF, with Buffer Soft AR-DPCBF providing the best overall trade-off between safety, feasibility, and robustness.
